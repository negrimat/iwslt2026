Balancing Linguistic Intelligibility and Speaker Identity in Zero-Shot Cross-Lingual Voice Cloning

Jamal uddin, Mo Ahtasam and Mohammad Nadeem

(Submission #12)

Summary

Cross-lingual voice cloning (CLVC) pushes the boundaries of speech synthesis by requiring the accurate transfer of a speaker's vocal identity such as timbre, prosody, and style across different languages with distinct phonetic structures. In this work, we present a systematic evaluation of four state-of-the-art models: MOSS-TTS (8B), Qwen3-TTS (1.7B), VoxCPM2 (0.8B), and CosyVoice3 (0.5B), submitted to the IWSLT 2026 Cross-Lingual Voice Cloning track. We evaluate zero-shot generalization by cloning English source speakers from the ACL-60/60 dataset into multiple target languages, including Arabic, Chinese, French, Korean, German, Russian, Portuguese, Spanish, and Italian. We analyze how different modeling approaches autoregressive language modeling and diffusion-based flow matching handle the tradeoff between speech accuracy and speaker identity preservation using multilingual representations and speaker-conditioned embeddings. Our results show that larger models like MOSS-TTS produce more stable and accurate speech over longer contexts, while smaller models like CosyVoice3 achieve strong speaker similarity with faster inference. We also conduct an ablation study to better understand how to improve zero-shot speaker identity preservation for applications such as assistive communication and multilingual content generation.
